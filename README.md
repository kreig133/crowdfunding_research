# Should you back the big board game now, or buy it later?

A large crowdfunding campaign can make a pledge feel like the last affordable chance to get a game. The resale record suggests a simpler rule: **if you are willing to buy used and wait, the typical copy gets cheaper.** A sealed copy soon after delivery usually costs more. Some games remain expensive, and a low resale price does not guarantee that a copy will be easy to find.

There are two ways to wait, and they buy different things. A **used Kickstarter-edition copy** can contain the same campaign extras and exclusives as a backer's copy. A **retail edition** may cost much less, but it may also leave those extras out. Compare the contents before comparing prices.

## What the sales show

The comparison uses **3,565 clean second-hand sales across 96 big crowdfunded tabletop games**, with core pledges around $79 or more. For 73 of the 96 games we also collected eBay listings, retail-edition sales, reprint history and delivery dates. The games are split by what comes in the priced core box: **57 miniatures games** have at least ten plastic or resin character or creature figures, and **39 other games** do not. Buildings, terrain, standees, tokens, wooden pieces and ship tokens do not count. The selection combines a hand-picked list of narrative and adventure games, campaigns tagged for miniatures, and a random sample of large tabletop campaigns; none of those selection routes determines the figure-count label. Each sale is compared with the original campaign cost of matched contents, in US dollars. The real-dollar comparison adjusts the earlier pledge for US inflation through the month of the sale. Shipping is excluded from both prices.

In the first year after estimated delivery, a used copy sold for a median **9% above** the inflation-adjusted original pledge. At one to two years, the median was **4% below**; at two to three years, **17% below**. Sealed copies stayed more expensive: their medians were **54% above** in the first year and **41% above** in the second. The 5+-year bins draw on only 20 games for used copies (132 sales) and 7 for sealed copies (34 sales); a few long-lived games dominate both, so the rebound is not a reason to wait longer.

![Median resale compared with the original pledge by years after delivery](charts/median_resale_by_year.svg)

Across all games, the share of used sales below the real pledge rose from **40%** in the first year to **54%** at one to two years, **64%** at two to three years, and **73%** at three to five years. A buyer looking for a particular game may still face a very different price.

![Share of used sales below the pledge by years after delivery](charts/used_below_cost.svg)

The two content groups differ in timing. The **57 miniatures games** have **1,886 clean sales**; their used copies were a median **20% above** the real pledge in the first year, **6% above** at one to two years, and **4% below** at two to three years. The **39 other games** have **1,679 sales**; their used copies were **6% above**, then **14% below**, then **25% below** in those same bands. Yet the median of game-level post-delivery results is about **1% below** for miniatures and **3% below** for others, with **29 of 57** and **23 of 39** games below cost. The time-band gaps describe these observed sales, not a guaranteed effect of the figures in a box; several games have thin data.

## Where you buy matters

The marketplace does not identify every seller's country. Sales listed in USD are a rough proxy for the US market. Across all games, the median of post-delivery game medians was **6% below** the real pledge for USD-listed sales, versus **1% below** when all listing currencies were included. The USD-listed group has **2,271 sales in 92 games**; the full group has **3,565 in 96**. At one to two years after delivery, used copies were a median **11% below** cost in USD listings and **4% below** across all currencies.

![Used resale in USD-listed sales compared with all currencies](charts/usd_vs_all.svg)

Recent eBay sold listings from July through September 2026 offer a separate check on these 73 games, using base games rather than matched bundles. Across **151 sales**, the median was **11% below** the original core pledge before inflation and **26% below** after inflation. That is a snapshot of recent listings, not a full price history or a result for the newly pooled groups.

## Does campaign size predict a bargain?

Only weakly. Among **66 games** with at least five USD-listed sales after delivery, the rank correlation between backer count and a game's median resale result was **+0.24**; a permutation test gave **p = .045**. Of the **17** games with fewer than 2,000 backers, **15** had medians below cost; the group's median was **15% below**. The larger groups did not rise steadily: the 2,000–9,999 group was **2% below**, the 10,000–24,999 group **13% above**, and the 25,000-plus group **14% below**. Among the **38 qualifying miniatures games** alone, the correlation was **+0.28**.

![Game resale medians plotted against campaign backers](charts/backers_vs_resale.svg)

The 2,000-backer dividing line was chosen after seeing the sales, and smaller campaigns tend to be newer. More backers also meant more USD-listed sales per year, with a **+0.38** rank correlation. The smallest group had a median of only **3.6 USD-listed sales per year**. Cheap and available are different things.

## Used campaign copy or retail box?

For nine of these 73 games that had enough sales of both editions, retail-edition copies sold a median **52% below** Kickstarter-edition copies within the same game; retail was cheaper in **six of nine**. Retail-edition sales were observed for about half of these 73 games. The large gaps usually accompany missing campaign extras or stretch goals. When the content was effectively identical, there was no clear discount. A retail box is therefore an option only if one exists and its contents suit you.

![Retail and Kickstarter edition resale results within the same game](charts/retail_vs_kickstarter.svg)

Reprints can change the price of a Kickstarter-edition copy too. Among these 73 games, resale prices fell after reprint campaigns even after accounting for a game's age. Publishers are more likely to reprint games with demand, so this pattern does not prove a reprint will happen for a particular game.

## A practical rule

If you want the campaign contents but can tolerate a used box, start looking about **1½–2 years after delivery**; for a miniatures game, allow **2–3 years or more** for a typical used discount. Compare the exact edition and included extras, and set a price alert early for small-run games. If you are happy with the retail contents, check whether a retail edition exists; it can be cheaper much sooner. Backing makes most sense when early access and a sealed copy matter enough to justify the higher typical price and the risk of delay or non-delivery.

The clock starts earlier for a backer. For these 73 games, delivery was roughly **two years after the campaign ended**, so buying a used copy below the real pledge often meant waiting **three to four years from the pledge date**. The backer is paying partly for earlier access.

These are observed resale prices, not guaranteed offers. Games that never arrived or stopped trading are underrepresented. Some sale bins contain few games or sales; listing currencies do not establish seller location. The analysis leaves out shipping on both sides and does not price unmatched add-ons. Inflation is measured with US CPI even for sales originally listed in other currencies. A current buyer should compare the actual delivered cost and contents of the available listing with the pledge they would have made.
